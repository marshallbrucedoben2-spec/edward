**Working title:** Witness Without a Pulpit: Religious Digital Testimony and the Slow Violence of Extraction in Raja Ampat

Ecological harm from mining rarely arrives as a single event. It accumulates as slow violence, dispersed in time and hard to report. This article examines how religious and indigenous voices used social media under the hashtag #SaveRajaAmpat to make such harm visible, and asks what kind of communication this was. It argues that the campaign is best understood not as advocacy alone but as testimony: speech in which a community stakes its credibility on what it has seen and asks others to take it on trust. Drawing on social epistemology of testimony and on public theology, the article analyses how religious language (creation, stewardship, lament) gave the testimony authority beyond its immediate audience, and where it was discounted. It proposes that digital religious testimony is a distinct genre of crisis communication, with its own conditions of credibility, and that churches in Indonesia can treat it as a form of public witness. The article contributes a Southeast Asian case to the study of religious communication under ecological crisis and connects it to debates on testimonial injustice.

**Keywords:** testimony; slow violence; digital religion; public theology; Raja Ampat; ecotheology
