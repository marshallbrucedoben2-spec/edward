**Working title:** Pancasila as a Public-Theological Settlement: Social Ethics after Religious Pluralism in Indonesia

Indonesia's founding philosophy, Pancasila, is usually read either as a civil religion that domesticates faith or as a secular frame that keeps religion out of public reason. This article argues for a third reading: Pancasila as a public-theological settlement, a negotiated agreement in which religious communities accepted shared terms of public life without giving up their own grounds for accepting them. Drawing on debates in public theology and on the social-ethical question of how plural societies justify common rules, the article reconstructs the settlement's founding compromise and asks what it requires of churches today. It then turns to an Indonesian ethical resource, sipakatau (mutual humanisation) from South Sulawesi, to show how a local moral vocabulary can carry the settlement beyond toleration towards shared responsibility. The article contributes to Christian social ethics a non-Western case in which theological reasons and public justification are held together rather than set against each other, and it proposes criteria for judging when a religious community's participation in such a settlement is faithful and when it is merely accommodating.

**Keywords:** Pancasila; public theology; social ethics; religious pluralism; Indonesia; sipakatau
