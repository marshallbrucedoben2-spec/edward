**Working title:** Calvin's Two Governments in a Pancasila State: Reformed Public Theology in Indonesia

Calvin's account of civil government in the final chapter of the Institutes has been read in Europe as a charter for Christian magistracy and, later, as a resource for constitutional resistance. Indonesian Reformed churches inherited this teaching inside a state that is neither Christian nor secular, but founded on Pancasila, a settlement in which belief in God is public while no single religion governs. This paper asks what Calvin's distinction between spiritual and political government means in that setting. Using Paul Ricoeur's hermeneutics of appropriation, it reads Institutes IV.20 alongside Indonesian Reformed engagement with the state and argues that Calvin's concern for an ordered common life supports a church that takes part in public reason without claiming it. The paper contributes an Asian case to the theme "Calvin, church, and society" and proposes that Calvin's political theology is best received in plural societies as a theology of public responsibility rather than of Christian rule.
