**Working title:** Minority Witness and the Defence of Plural Democracy: Pancasila, Public Theology, and Christian Responses to Majoritarianism in Indonesia

Studies of Christianity and democratic erosion mostly examine settings where Christians form the majority. This article turns to Indonesia, where Christians are a minority and democratic pressure comes from religious majoritarianism. It asks what public theology looks like when the church cannot claim the centre and must defend a plural settlement it did not design alone. Reading Pancasila as a public-theological settlement, the article argues that Indonesian churches resist anti-democratic projects best not by building counter-majorities but by keeping open the shared terms on which every community can give public reasons. It draws on liberation theology's critique of pacification to distinguish such witness from quiet accommodation. The case widens the special issue's scope beyond the North Atlantic and shows how minority churches can serve as custodians of democratic pluralism.

**Keywords:** public theology; Pancasila; democracy; religious majoritarianism; Indonesia; liberation theology
